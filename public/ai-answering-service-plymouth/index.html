Why Plymouth trades miss calls

Plymouth takes the weather straight off the Atlantic. Wind-driven rain finds every gap in a roof, a chimney or a window frame, and salt air on the waterfront wears out anything metal faster than it would inland. After a proper storm, roofers, builders and anyone who does damp and leaks will have their phones ringing for days.

The housing adds to it. Much of the city centre was rebuilt after the Blitz, so there's a lot of post-war construction now reaching the age where it needs real work. Around Mutley, Peverell, Stoke and Keyham there are long streets of Victorian terraces. Devonport has older naval housing. Out at Plympton, Plymstock and Ivybridge it's mostly newer estates. Every kind of job, and plenty of it.

Both sides of the Tamar

A lot of Plymouth trades work across the Tamar Bridge in Saltash, Torpoint and further into Cornwall. Those callers have PL postcodes too, but they're a different drive, and the bridge toll and traffic make a difference to how you plan your day.

The agent takes the full address and postcode and reads it back, so you can see at a glance whether a job is in PL4 in the city or PL12 across the river. If you don't cross the bridge, I'll set it up to tell Cornish callers that before they wait for a callback.

Keeping your 01752 number

Your number doesn't change. Whether customers ring a 01752 landline or your mobile, your phone diverts the calls you don't answer to Pulsemint after however many rings you choose. Your van, your website, your Google Business Profile and your Checkatrade or MyBuilder listing all stay exactly as they are.

Setup takes about ten minutes of your time. I set the agent up with your business name, the trades you do, the Plymouth areas you cover and anywhere you won't travel to. If anything ever fails at our end, calls ring your phone as they do now.
