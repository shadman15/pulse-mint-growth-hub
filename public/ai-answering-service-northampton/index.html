Why Northampton trades miss calls

The town has two very different kinds of housing. The streets around the old boot and shoe quarter, and areas like Abington and Semilong, are full of terraces built when Northampton made shoes for half the country. Then there's everything built during the new town expansion from the late 1960s onwards, across the Eastern District estates like Lings, Lumbertubs and Thorplands, and south of the river at East and West Hunsbury.

Both keep trades busy. Old terraces need rewires, new pipework and roof repairs. The 1970s and 80s estates are on their second or third boiler and their original windows and fascias are going. Add newer developments like Upton, and there's no shortage of work. There is a shortage of time to answer the phone while you're doing it.

Getting Northampton addresses right

Callers in Northampton often give the estate rather than the street, and some estates share names. East Hunsbury and West Hunsbury are on opposite sides of the A45. Grange Park sits just outside the town boundary. Duston, Kingsthorpe, Weston Favell, Moulton and Wootton are all distinct, and callers from the surrounding villages may say "Northampton" when they mean somewhere half an hour out.

Pulsemint takes the full address and postcode and reads it back. If you only cover certain areas, I set it up so it can tell callers further out that you don't travel that far, which saves both of you a wasted call.

Close to the motorway, far from your phone

With the M1 junctions on the edge of town and warehouses and offices around Brackmills, plenty of Northampton trades also do commercial work. Facilities managers ring during office hours and expect a quick response. They aren't going to wait a day for a callback when there are other contractors on their list.

Only your own numbers can answer that. Count last week's missed calls, take out the spam and suppliers, and be realistic about how many were jobs you'd have won.
