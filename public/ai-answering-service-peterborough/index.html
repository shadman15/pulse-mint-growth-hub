Why Peterborough trades miss calls

Peterborough grew fast as a new town from the 1970s, and that growth hasn't stopped. There are the original townships like Bretton, Werrington and the Ortons, Hampton to the south which is still filling up, and newer developments on the edges. Thousands of homes built within a few decades of each other means a lot of them need the same work at the same time: boilers reaching the end of their life, consumer units being upgraded, bathrooms being refitted.

There's also a big rental market, with plenty of landlords and letting agents who want certificates done and repairs sorted quickly. They ring during the working day, when you're on site.

The Orton problem

Ask someone in Peterborough where they live, and "Orton" isn't an answer. There's Orton Goldhay, Orton Malborne, Orton Longueville, Orton Waterville, Orton Northgate and Orton Brimbles, and they aren't next to each other in the way the names suggest. Hampton has the same issue, with Hampton Vale, Hampton Hargate and Hampton Centre.

An answering service that writes down "Orton" and a street name has given you half an address. Pulsemint asks for the full postcode and reads it back, so you know exactly where you're going. A PE2 postcode in the Ortons is a different drive from a PE7 one in Hampton, and you'll know which before you ring back.

New builds and snagging

A lot of Peterborough's newer homes are still within their first few years, which means snagging calls, questions about what the builder's warranty covers, and first-time buyers who've never had to find a tradesperson before. Those callers are often anxious and ring several numbers. Getting a clear message from your business, and a callback the same day, wins a lot of that work.
