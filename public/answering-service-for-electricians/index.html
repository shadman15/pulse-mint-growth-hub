Why electricians miss so many calls

Safe isolation means you can't drop everything because the phone rings. You're halfway through testing a circuit, in a loft pulling cable, or with your hands in a consumer unit. Picking up isn't an option, and it shouldn't be.

The trouble is the mix of calls you get. Some are emergencies, like no power or a breaker that keeps tripping. Some are valuable quotes: a rewire, a consumer unit upgrade, an EV charger. And some are compliance work from landlords who need an EICR before a deadline. Miss any of them and the caller rings the next electrician on Google.

What Pulsemint asks on an electrical call

For faults and no power

  • Is the whole property off, or just one circuit?
  • Are the neighbours off too? If they are, it's probably a power cut, and the agent tells the caller to ring 105, the free national power cut number. That saves you a wasted callback.
  • Is something tripping? Which breaker or RCD, and does it trip straight away or after a while?
  • Any burning smell, scorch marks or buzzing? You decide what it tells callers in that case. Most electricians have it tell them to switch off at the main switch if it's safe and mark the call urgent.

For quotes

  • What the job is: rewire, consumer unit change, extra sockets, outdoor lighting, EV charger, solar or battery
  • Property type, age and number of bedrooms, which tells you a lot about a rewire before you visit
  • For EV chargers, whether they have off-street parking and roughly how far the driveway is from the fuse board
  • When they want it done

For landlords and letting agents

  • How many properties need an EICR, and when the current reports expire
  • How to arrange access with tenants
  • Who the invoice goes to

What it won't do

It won't quote a price, promise a date or agree to anything on your behalf. Those are your decisions, and an agent that guesses at them creates problems. It tells the caller you'll ring them back, and if someone asks whether they're talking to an AI, it says yes straight away.

Letting agent calls are worth more than they look

In England, private landlords need an Electrical Installation Condition Report at least every five years under the 2020 private rented sector regulations. Letting agents manage dozens or hundreds of properties, and when they find an electrician who answers and turns reports round, they keep using them.

One missed call from a letting agent might be three EICRs this month and a steady stream of remedial work after that. Those are exactly the calls that come in during the working day, when you're least able to pick up.

Registration and sign-off questions

Callers often ask whether you're registered or whether you can sign off work under Part P. If you're NICEIC or NAPIT registered, the agent can say so. Anything more detailed, like whether a particular job is notifiable, it leaves for you to answer when you ring back, because getting that wrong on the phone helps nobody.

Does it pay for itself?

Use your own numbers, not anybody's industry statistics. Open your phone and count last week's missed calls. Take out the suppliers, the spam and the people who rang back later. Be realistic about how many of the rest you'd have won, then multiply by what a job is worth to you.

Electrical work varies a lot in value. A single rewire is often worth more than a year of Pulsemint, and a letting agent who becomes a regular customer is worth more again. If you miss even one of those a quarter, the service has paid for itself.

If the sum comes out small, you probably don't need an answering service. A better voicemail greeting and missed call text back will do. I'd rather you knew that before you sign up for anything. If you want to put your own figures in, the calculator on the Pulsemint homepage does the sum for you.

Setting it up

You keep your number. The number on your van, your website and your Google listing doesn't change. Your phone diverts the calls you don't answer, after however many rings you choose, and Pulsemint picks up instead of voicemail. Most UK networks let you set that divert in a couple of minutes, and I'll walk you through it for yours.

The setup itself takes about ten minutes of your time. I ask what you cover, which areas you work in, what you never want it to say, and what counts as urgent for you. Then I build the agent and you ring it yourself to test it before a single customer hears it.

If anything ever goes wrong at our end, the divert fails safe: calls ring your mobile exactly as they do today. You're never worse off than you are now.

Common questions

Can it tell a power cut from a fault?

It asks whether the neighbours have lost power too. If they have, it tells the caller to ring 105, the national power cut number, and lets you know in the summary so you don't waste a callback.

What does it say if someone smells burning?

Whatever you decide when we set it up. Most electricians have it tell the caller to switch off at the main switch if it's safe to do so, and mark the call urgent so the text reaches you first.

Can it handle EICR enquiries from letting agents?

Yes. It takes the number of properties, postcodes, expiry dates, access arrangements and who pays, so you can quote without ringing back to ask.

Will it say I'm NICEIC or NAPIT registered?

Yes, if you are and you want it to. It won't answer detailed regulatory questions, such as whether a job is notifiable, and leaves those for you.

Does it work at evenings and weekends?

Yes, at the same price. A lot of homeowners only ring about quotes in the evening, after work.

Do I have to change my number?

No. Calls you don't answer divert to Pulsemint, and your existing number stays on everything.
