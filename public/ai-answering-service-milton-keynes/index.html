Why Milton Keynes trades miss calls

Milton Keynes has grown faster than almost any other place in the UK since it was designated a new town in 1967, and it's still growing. The earliest grid squares are now fifty years old, which means their boilers, roofs, windows and wiring are due. Newer areas like Brooklands, Whitehouse and Fairfields are full of first-time buyers who need a tradesperson for the first time.

That's a lot of work for local trades, and a lot of calls. Most of them arrive while you're already on a job.

Grid roads, estates and addresses

In most towns, a caller gives you a street name. In Milton Keynes, they'll usually give you an estate: Oldbrook, Kents Hill, Shenley Church End, Broughton, Oxley Park. Directions get given in grid roads, like "off H6 Childs Way" or "near V8 Marlborough Street", rather than street names. Then there are the older towns that were absorbed into MK and still have their own identity: Bletchley, Wolverton, Stony Stratford, Newport Pagnell.

An answering service that only writes down "Shenley" has missed the difference between Shenley Church End, Shenley Lodge, Shenley Brook End and Shenley Wood. Pulsemint asks for the full postcode and reads the address back, so you know exactly which grid square you're heading to before you ring.

One thing to be upfront about: Pulsemint is run from Lowestoft, not Milton Keynes. The agent answers calls remotely, so where it's based makes no difference to the caller. What matters is whether it gets Milton Keynes addresses right, and whether you can reach a person when you want something changed. You can: you deal with me directly.
