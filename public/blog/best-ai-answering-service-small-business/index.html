The best AI answering service for small businesses: an honest 2026 comparison

By Shadman Chowdhury, founder of Pulsemint · Published 30 October 2026 · About 10 minutes to read

I build one of the services in this comparison, so read it with that in mind. I have tried to write the guide I wanted when I started: real prices, honest trade-offs, and a straight answer about who each one actually suits.

If you run a small business and your phone goes unanswered while you work, you already know the problem. You are up a ladder, under a sink, with a client, or driving between jobs. The phone rings. You cannot get to it.

Here is the part that costs money. Most people who ring a business and get voicemail do not leave a message. They hang up and ring the next name on the list. You never hear about the job at all, so it never feels like a loss. It just feels like a quiet week.

An AI answering service is one way to stop that. This guide covers what these services actually do, how to judge them, what five of them cost right now, and how to test one properly before you hand over a card.

What an AI answering service actually does

It answers the calls you cannot take. A caller hears a voice, has a short conversation, and the service collects the things you need: who they are, their number, what the job is, and where. Within a minute or so, that arrives as a text or an email. You ring back when you are free.

That is the core of it. Most services then add extras: booking into your calendar, answering common questions about opening hours and areas covered, sending the caller a text, or passing urgent calls through to your mobile.

What it is not is a replacement for you. The agent should not be quoting prices or promising dates. Those are your decisions, and an agent that guesses at them creates a problem rather than solving one. The job of the agent is to make sure the caller is dealt with politely and that you get a lead you can act on.

The seven questions that decide which one you need

Most comparison articles list features. Features are not the issue. These seven questions are what actually separate the services:

  1. Who sets it up? Some services hand you a dashboard and a blank script. Others configure it for you. If you are not comfortable writing out what the agent should say in a dozen situations, that difference matters more than price.
  2. How are you charged? Per minute, per call, per customer handled, or a flat monthly fee. Per-minute pricing punishes chatty callers. Flat pricing is easier to budget for.
  3. What happens when you go over? Check the overage rate before you sign up, not after. This is where a cheap plan stops being cheap.
  4. Does it handle your accents and place names? A service trained mostly on American callers can struggle with UK postcodes, street names and regional accents. Test this specifically.
  5. Where does the lead land? A text to your phone is useful. A notification inside an app you never open is not.
  6. Can you reach a human when it breaks? It will need adjusting in the first fortnight. Find out now who you will be talking to.
  7. What does it say when a caller asks if it is a robot? Ask the provider directly. The honest answer is that it should confirm it, straight away, without hedging.

AI or a human answering service?

The honest answer is that it depends on what your calls are like.

A human service is better when your callers are often distressed, your work involves sensitive or regulated conversations, or the person ringing needs real judgement rather than accurate note-taking. People are still better at reading a caller who is upset.

An AI service is better when most of your calls are straightforward enquiries, you need cover at three in the morning and on Sundays, and your volume is uneven. It does not take holidays, does not mind a quiet week, and costs the same whether it takes two calls or forty.

There is a third option people forget: use both. Let the agent take everything, and route the handful of calls that need a person through to you or a colleague while you are available.

The simple maths on whether it pays

Forget industry statistics. Use your own numbers, because they are the only ones that matter.

Open your phone and count the missed calls from last week. Most owners guess two or three and find more. Then ask yourself two questions: what fraction of those were people who wanted work doing, and what is a job worth to you on average?

If you miss five calls a week, two of those were real enquiries, you would win half of them, and a job is worth £300 to you, that is roughly £300 a week walking away.

Against that, a service at £50 or £200 a month is an easy decision. If the same sum gives you one enquiry a fortnight worth £80, it is not. Do the sum before you shop, not after. "My customers will hate talking to a robot"

This is the objection I hear most, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a reassurance.

Some callers will not love it. That is true. But the comparison that matters is not AI against you. When you are on a job, the caller does not get you either way. The comparison is AI against a voicemail beep.

Given that choice, most people would rather speak to something that asks what the job is and takes their number than leave a message into silence and wonder if anyone will ring back. The bar is not whether the agent sounds human. The bar is whether the caller is dealt with properly and you get the job.

Two things make the difference between an agent people tolerate and one they resent. It should tell the truth when asked whether it is an AI, immediately and without waffle. And it should never invent a price or a date, because the caller will hold you to it.

How to test one in fifteen minutes

Every service here has a trial or a demo. Do not judge it by the marketing video. Ring it yourself and try to break it:

  • Speak normally. Use your actual accent, at your actual speed. Do not slow down for it.
  • Add noise. Ring from a van with the engine running, or a site with a radio on. That is how your customers will ring.
  • Give a difficult address. A local street name or an awkward postcode. See if it gets it right.
  • Interrupt it. Talk over it mid-sentence, the way real people do.
  • Ask for a price. A good agent declines politely and says you will confirm. An agent that guesses is a liability.
  • Ask if it is a robot. You want a clear yes.
  • Then check your inbox. This is the real test. Did the name, number and job description arrive, and are they right? Could you ring that person back without needing to ask everything again?

Do that with two services and the choice usually makes itself.

Common questions

What is an AI answering service?

Software that answers your phone when you cannot, has a short spoken conversation with the caller, collects their details and what they need, and sends that to you as a text or email. It runs day and night.

How much does one cost?

Self-serve AI services run from roughly $29 to $299 a month depending on included minutes, with per-minute charges after that. Done-for-you services cost more because somebody else does the configuring. UK human answering services start around £45 a month and rise with volume.

Will it work with my existing phone number?

In most cases yes. The usual setup diverts your number to the service when you do not answer within a few rings, so customers carry on ringing the number they already have. Check this with any provider before you sign up.

Is it better than voicemail?

For most small businesses, yes, because many callers will not leave a voicemail at all. An agent that takes a name, a number and a description of the job gives you something to ring back.

Can it book appointments?

Several can connect to a calendar and book directly. Whether you want that is a separate question. Plenty of owners prefer the agent to take details and leave the scheduling to them, because only they know how long a job will really take.

What happens if it mishears something?

It will, occasionally, same as a person. What matters is that you get a recording or a transcript so you can check, and that the caller's number is captured correctly so you can always ring back and ask.
